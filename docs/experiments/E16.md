# E16 · Distillation and on-device deployment

Written before the experiment runs (PLAN 8.1-8.5); the Results section is added afterwards.
The GPU part runs in the combined Phases 6-8 kit (ADR-027); latency and the tables run on the
laptop afterwards.

**Scope change (ADR-029, 2026-10-25, before any run):** students are distilled at T = 4 only (`kd-t4`, 30 runs); the T = 2 arm is dropped. The deployment pass covers the 30 students and the 15 final-model fold models.

## Question

How much accuracy and reliability does a phone-sized model keep when distilled from the final
model and quantized to INT8?

## Hypotheses

- Distilled students lose little pooled OOF AUROC against the final model.
- ~~Distillation beats training the same student with cross-entropy only.~~ (Dropped before any run with the `ce` arm, ADR-027 amendment, 2026-10-25.)
- INT8 quantization changes AUROC by less than 0.01 and shrinks the model about 4x.

## Arms (full protocol; default recipe, full fine-tuning)

| Arm | Runs |
|---|---|
| MobileNetV3-Large, EfficientNet-Lite0, distilled at T = 2 (`kd-t2`) and T = 4 (`kd-t4`) | 60 |

Loss: 0.5 BCE(student, y) + 0.5 T^2 KL(teacher_T || student_T), two-class tempered sigmoids.
Teacher: the final model's fold model of the same fold and seed (ADR-027), in eval mode, on the
same augmented batch. A cross-entropy-only reference arm (`ce`, 30 runs) was planned and dropped
before any run to save GPU time (ADR-027 amendment, 2026-10-25); the arms are those of PLAN 8.1.

## Deployment pass (`deploy/`; every student fold model and the 15 final-model fold models)

- ONNX export (`torch.onnx.export`, TorchScript exporter, opset 17; LoRA merged), accepted
  when the largest |logit difference| against PyTorch FP32 on the fold's `val` images is at most
  1e-4.
- Static INT8 (ONNX Runtime, QDQ, per-channel QInt8 weights, QUInt8 activations, MinMax) on 200
  training images of the fold (drawn with the run seed).
- FP32 and INT8 predictions on `val`, `cal` and held-out images (CPU) -> temperature,
  threshold and conformal quantiles re-fitted per exported model on the laptop (PLAN 8.3; E10
  methods and the conformal method chosen in E10).
- Latency on the laptop (PLAN 8.4, `deploy/latency.py`): median of 100 runs after 10 warm-ups,
  batch 1; phone browser via ONNX Runtime Web (device and browser recorded).

## Metrics

Pooled OOF AUROC (CI) per model x precision, DeLong vs the final model; ECE after temperature;
conformal coverage and mean set size; size (MB); latency (ms).

## Planned tables and figures

- `results/tables/e16_deployment.{csv,md}`: AUROC, ECE, coverage, size MB, latency ms.
- `results/figures/e16_pareto.{png,pdf}`: AUROC vs latency.

## Done criterion

60 complete `e16_*` runs; `deploy.json` and `deploy_predictions.csv` in those and the 15
final-model run folders; latency measured on the laptop.

## GPU-hour estimate

Student jobs are expected to take about as long as a final-model job (1.09 min; the teacher adds
one forward pass per batch): 60 x 1.1 min = 1.1 h. Deployment pass on the CPU: about 1 min per
fold model, about 1.3 h for 75.

## Findings

neither student is significantly different from the final model in pooled OOF AUROC (Holm p 0.4759 for both); EfficientNet-Lite0 has a higher ECE and log-loss than the final model (CIs above 0). INT8 shrinks every model about 3.5x; the pooled held-out AUROC of the INT8 MobileNetV3 is 0.0033 below its FP32 version, the other two change by less than that. After re-fitting, overall conformal coverage at alpha 0.10 is 0.907-0.923 for every model and precision (lowest per-class mean 0.890, Lite0 FP32 authentic); the INT8 MobileNetV3 has the highest re-fitted ECE (0.1014 ± 0.0659).

Anomalies: the FP32 ONNX check (max |ONNX - PyTorch| <= 1e-4 on val) failed in 2 of 15 fold models per student (largest difference 4.79e-04 Lite0, 1.63e-04 MobileNetV3); the final model passed 15/15. The failed exports were still quantized and scored; their predictions are in the tables. Latency (PLAN 8.4) is not measured yet: the ONNX files come in the MODELS bundle, which was not on the drive.
